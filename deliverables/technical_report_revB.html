IGBT Test Cell Technical Report

Simulink/Simscape engineering core of a pre-installation IGBT and gate-driver test cell: what the simulations show, what they do not show, and the proposed service procedure.

0Verdict

Numerical model validationPASSKCL, energy balance and half-step convergence closed
Hardware health decisionINCONCLUSIVEneeds the real Wabtec part numbers, approved limits and measured modules

The project contains two executable Simscape Electrical experiments built on a representative 3.3 kV / 1500 A traction IGBT (Mitsubishi CM1500HC-66XB, not a confirmed Wabtec part):

  • an RL double-pulse test at 1800 V and about 1.5 kA, recording gate, collector, load, diode and bus quantities;
  • a closed-loop DESAT short-circuit test, in which the measured VCE passes a blanking, comparator and latch chain that removes the gate command.

The strongest result is an internally consistent electrical experiment that keeps numerical validation separate from hardware acceptance. It does not prove that any real IGBT is healthy.

2RL double-pulse model

Circuit: ideal 1800 V source → Ibus sensor → IL sensor → load R 0.01 Ω → load L 300 µH → switch node → stray inductance 100 nH → IC sensor → IGBT → ground. The freewheel diode (with an explicit 11 nF capacitance) connects the switch node back to the bus side of the load. A Simscape Gate Driver (+15 V / −15 V) drives the gate.

Why 1800 V, 300 µH and 250 µs. The current slope during the first pulse is about V/L:

di/dt ≈ 1800 V / 300 µH ≈ 6 A/µs → ΔI ≈ 6 A/µs × 250 µs ≈ 1500 A

So the first pulse (50–300 µs) is sized to reach the 1.5 kA rating of the representative device; the simulation reaches 1479 A at 299 µs.

The energies are gross collector-terminal integrals over fixed windows. They include conduction and reactive transfer and are not Eon/Eoff as defined in the datasheet.

Turn-off tail. The modelled current fall (≈ 22 µs, 10–90 %) is far longer than the datasheet maximum tf 0.70 µs (1800 V, 1500 A, Tj 150 °C; the model runs at 25 °C). The tail parameter is assumed; three calibration attempts stopped the solver, so simulated turn-off timing is not a physical prediction.

Gate charge. Integrating IG over the second turn-on (400 µs, ≈ 1.6 kA) gives QG ≈ 7.44 µC, about 9 % below the datasheet typical 8.2 µC (1800 V, 1500 A, ±15 V, 25 °C). This is a consistency indication, not a calibration.

4Closed-loop DESAT short circuit

Type-I short circuit: the gate is switched on into a short across the 1800 V link (only the 100 nH stray inductance limits the current), the case slide 8 describes as a failed companion IGBT. The DC link is first ramped from 10 V to 1800 V over 8 µs, because starting the network in the 1800 V equilibrium stalled the solver.

FaultNow = (V_CE > 9 V) AND BlankingDone(7.5 µs) ; Latch = FaultNow OR Latch_previous ; GateEnable = Command AND NOT Latch

Settings (all assumed): threshold 9 V, blanking 7.5 µs, detector sampling 50 ns, gate command at 20 µs for 30 µs, stop at 70 µs. The 7.5 µs blanking follows from the healthy double pulse, where VCE needed just over 6.99 µs to fall below 9 V; 5 µs blanking false-tripped. It is a simulation choice, not a Wabtec value.

Peak VCE after the trip 1947.4 V; short-circuit terminal energy 434.5 J; half-step change 0.04 %. The validation JSON measures from the 20.00 µs command edge (trip 7.60 µs, IC < 10 % at 32.02 µs); the CSV gate command appears one 50 ns sample later.

The slide 8 notes count the 10 µs from fault detection. The protection logic and driver pull the gate below threshold 1.94 µs after detection, but with the modelled tail the current is below 10 % only 24.4 µs after detection. The run therefore demonstrates protection-loop function, not 10 µs current clearing.

The 17.4 kA peak is a model value outside the range the model was calibrated on; it is not an expected real short-circuit current and says nothing about device survival. The datasheet tpSC of 10 µs (VCC ≤ 2500 V, ±15 V, Ls ≤ 100 nH, Tj = Tjop) is a withstand rating, not the Wabtec detection-to-turn-off requirement. At gate-on, VGE had settled only to about −14.3 V after the DC-link ramp (Miller coupling); this does not change the result.

Acceptance limits

The representative datasheet values (VGE(th) 5.8–6.8 V, IGES ±0.5 µA at ±20 V, ICES ≤ 3 mA at 3300 V and 25 °C, VCE(sat) ≤ 3.70 V at 1500 A and 150 °C) can check the simulation, but they are not Wabtec criteria. Slide values (±15 V gate, diode 0.2–0.8 V and reverse open, ≈ 10 µs protection, driver ≤ 20 W, busbar leakage ≤ 2 mA) still lack test conditions and tolerances. A missing limit must never become PASS:

  • PASS: every mandatory result is valid and inside an approved limit.
  • NO-PASS: a valid measurement violates an approved limit.
  • INCONCLUSIVE / NOT VERIFIED: part number, test condition, approved limit or valid measurement is missing.

Three-minute service sequence (target)

Benchmark from the brief's speaker notes: about 3.5 minutes per IGBT with PASS / NO-PASS (Wabtec instruction IM-LE-0008, step 14). A 180 s target is credible only with a keyed fixture and automated measurements.

The switching experiment itself lasts 1.5 ms; service time is set by fixture handling, safe-state checks, settling, discharge and reporting. In service, the protection test should use an injected or strongly current-limited fault and record tdetect, tlatch, tgate-off and, where meaningful, tcurrent-clear separately, rather than a full 1800 V hard short on every module.

6Test-cell concept and cost

Four domains: low-energy power stimulus; gate-driver interface (actual driver, command / status, gate output); isolated measurement of VBUS, VCE, VGE, IC and status; hard-wired safety (contactor K1, E-stop, door interlock, discharge verification). The laptop sequences the test and writes the report; it is never the only safety layer. K1 closes only when the door is closed, E-stop healthy, link discharged, fixture locked and no instrument fault is present. The gate driver is tested before any power stage is enabled.

Catalogue prices as gathered by the reviewer in October 2026; confirm before presenting. A two-channel scope is a budget anchor: a production cell needs at least four simultaneous isolated fast channels (VGE, VCE, IC, status). An 80 V stimulus cannot show the slide 5 di/dt range at working voltage; a full 1.8 kV engineering rig (HV source or capacitor bank, precharge and discharge, pulse-rated inductor, HV contactors, rated probes, containment) is quote-based and outside the service-cell budget. Insulation testing needs a separate qualified path; whether a 1000 V insulation tester suffices for the busbar requirement is not known from the brief.

The wiring above is an engineering architecture, not a fabrication drawing. Creepage and clearance, fuse and contactor ratings, protective earth, conductor sizing, containment, EMC, calibration and safety approval (for example under IEC 61010-1) belong to the physical design phase.

  1. Obtain the exact phase-module and gate-driver part numbers, pinout, gate conditions, insulation requirement, protection timing definition and service acceptance criteria from Wabtec.
  2. Measure known-good and known-failed modules: VF, VGE(th), IGE, C–E leakage, VGE on / off, driver and DESAT timing, plus a normalised low-energy VCE(t), IC(t), VGE(t) signature.
  3. Set limits as OEM specification plus measurement uncertainty, from the measured distributions.
  4. Compare each bench result with a qualified Wabtec procedure or the known locomotive outcome; report false-pass and false-fail rates.
  5. Time the real three-minute procedure with technicians; the phase module weighs about 25 kg.

8Recommended claim

We developed and numerically validated the electrical core of a pre-installation IGBT test cell. The simulation demonstrates inductive switching, current commutation, measurement acquisition and closed-loop DESAT protection. On that basis we propose an automated service cell that checks the IGBT and its gate driver in about three minutes and reports PASS, NO-PASS or INCONCLUSIVE. Acceptance limits will be set from actual Wabtec modules and OEM data.

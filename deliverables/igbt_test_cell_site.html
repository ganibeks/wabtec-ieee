03 — EVIDENCE

Double pulse and short circuit

The double pulse builds the inductor current to 1.5 kA (≈ 6 A/µs × 250 µs) and hard-switches against the diode. The short-circuit run switches the module into a short across 1800 V with closed-loop DESAT protection.

04 — THE TEST CELL

One module, driven like the locomotive drives it

The module under test keeps its own integrated gate driver. The cell sends fiber-optic commands like the traction motor controller, powers the driver from a 100 VAC / 25 kHz emulator, and records VCE, IC and status through isolated channels.

10 sDiode checkforward drop and reverse open, low voltage
20 sGate and threshold±15 V levels and VGE(th) sweep
70 sInsulationbusbar hipot, 60 s dwell
30 sSwitching and protectioncharge, double pulse, DESAT trip, discharge, report

About 2–3 minutes of connected time per IGBT (estimate), against today's benchmark of about 3.5 minutes per IGBT. Module handling comes on top.

05 — HONEST LIMITS

What the simulation shows, and what it does not

Shown

  • Switching, commutation and the double-pulse sequence at 1800 V
  • Energy and current balance closed; results unchanged at half time step
  • DESAT detection, latch and gate shut-off in a closed loop
  • An automated verdict that never turns a missing check into PASS

Not shown

  • That a real Wabtec module or driver is healthy: hardware decision stays inconclusive
  • Current clearing within 10 µs: the model's turn-off tail is not calibrated
  • Diode reverse recovery, self-heating, real fiber and driver electronics
  • Acceptance limits: they need Wabtec part numbers and measured modules
